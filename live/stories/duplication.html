Philips India/2019/Engineering

Could teams find and remove duplicate code themselves?

I taught teams to use a detector. The engineers chose the changes and reported the lines they removed.

How we know +

The decision

Measure duplication with a free tool teams can run themselves, and pair every report with a hands-on session.

My contribution

Introduced the tool, wrote the installation and usage guides, designed the workshop and the before-and-after reporting template, ran sessions from September 2019 and compiled the results for leadership.

What changed

Developers across at least six business units ran the tool and reported back; a senior director asked for a mechanism to remove 23% of duplicate code; teams I influenced removed 18,688 duplicate lines.

Owner-reported in the 2019 review.

One artifact · three editorial readings

What can you learn from this evidence?

The approved public derivative is one engineer’s message with two report attachments. These annotations are readings of the artifact, not quotations.

  1. 1

    The change made

    In the message body, the engineer reports removing about 200 duplicate lines after running the detector on a project. This is one engineer’s example, not the program-wide total.

  2. 2

    The work made repeatable

    The same message describes roughly 15 minutes to set up the tool and about 10 seconds to analyze a 15k-line code base. That helps explain how teams could run their own analysis; it is not a measure of total program savings.

  3. 3

    The evidence attached

    At the bottom of the message are separate before and after report files. The attachments let a reviewer inspect this example; they do not establish the 18,688-line program figure by themselves.
